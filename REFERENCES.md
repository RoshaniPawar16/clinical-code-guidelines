# REFERENCES.md

---

**A critical moment in machine learning in medicine: on reproducible and interpretable learning**
Ciobanu-Caraus O, Aicher A, Kernbach JM, Regli L, Serra C, Staartjes VE. 2024. Acta Neurochirurgica.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10791964/
Supports Principle 1: of 511 clinical ML papers from 2017–2019, only 21% shared their analysis code and only 44% reported variance in their performance metrics.

---

**Design and implementation of an audit trail in compliance with US regulations**
Jiang K, Cao X. 2011. Clinical Trials.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21900341/
Supports Principle 2: meeting FDA 21 CFR Part 11 requires audit capture at the database level — capturing both data access and changes with the correct user identifier — application-layer logging alone is not sufficient.

---

**Second opinion needed: communicating uncertainty in medical machine learning**
Kompa B, Snoek J, Beam AL. 2021. npj Digital Medicine.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-020-00367-3
Supports Principle 4: four of the most widely cited medical ML models published since 2016 had no mechanism for abstention when uncertain; bootstrap analysis showed two patients with similar predicted risks (55% and 65%) had standard deviations of 7.6% and 15.3%, demonstrating how point estimates hide meaningful uncertainty.

---

**Linguistically Communicating Uncertainty in Patient-Facing Risk Prediction Models**
Sivaprasad A, Reiter E. 2024. UncertainLP Workshop, EACL.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.17511
Supports Principle 4: identifies four distinct challenges in communicating model uncertainty to patients in IVF outcome prediction — conveying model performance, confidence levels, reasoning, and unknown information.
